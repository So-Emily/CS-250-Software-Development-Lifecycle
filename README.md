---

## 💭 Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

When I think about user needs, I try to focus on what the user is actually trying to do, not just what features I think should be built. It’s really about understanding their goal first, then building something that supports that goal.

User stories helped me a lot with this because they break everything down into simple, clear pieces from the user’s perspective. Instead of getting overwhelmed, I can focus on one small goal at a time and make sure it actually provides value. It keeps development more organized and user-focused instead of just coding randomly.

---

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I usually approach development by breaking things into smaller steps and building gradually instead of trying to do everything at once. This course really reinforced that mindset with Agile.

I liked working in sprints and using retrospectives because it gave me a chance to reflect and improve instead of just moving on. In the future, I definitely want to keep using iterative development, sprint-style planning, and regular check-ins (even if I’m working solo). It just makes everything feel more manageable and less overwhelming.

---

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member means being reliable, communicating clearly, and being willing to collaborate. It’s not just about doing your own work—it’s about making sure the whole team succeeds.

That also means being open to feedback and helping others when needed. In Agile especially, communication is huge, so staying engaged and contributing to discussions really matters.

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways
This course helped me understand how useful Agile and Scrum can be in real development. I learned how important it is to break work into smaller pieces, stay flexible, and reflect on progress. It also showed me how structure (like sprints and retrospectives) can actually make development less stressful.
